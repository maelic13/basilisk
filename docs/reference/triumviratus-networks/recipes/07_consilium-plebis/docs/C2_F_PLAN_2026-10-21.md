# Consilium Plebis: plan of the F phase (21/10/2026)

> **What ran (27/10/2026):** P was stopped after epoch 496 and F started from it with the author's piecewise LR
> schedule (70 epochs, up to 25% of P's peak, a flat stretch at 15%, then down to P's final LR), one lambda 0.75 and
> the data of P. The data weighting by expert pair and the lambda per expert below were not used: the lambda
> calibration of 26/10 found one global mismatch, the same in every expert. Recipe and reasons:
> `../README.md` (Data and recipe) and `../LOG_RUN.md` (27/10). The plan below is kept as written.

F starts from the last P checkpoint (P ends around 28/10). Recipe as Consilium's F3, the phase that gave the Elo
(05_consilium2/README.md): same batch as P, peak lr 20% of P's, warmup 1%, lambda 0.75 without jitter,
`--router-em-epochs 0`.

## Decisions (author, 21/10/2026)

- **Idea 3 of C2_FABLE_IDEAS_2026-10-21.md: done in F.** A sampling weight per expert pair, from the distribution
  the search evaluates against the one the training stream gives, plus a lambda per expert of the side to move.
- **Idea 2 (search-weighted routing EM): not done.** The EM moves a cell only if the loss improves at once, judged
  by an expert that never trained on that cell; the experts are specialised on their cells since epoch 0, so the
  rounds gain ~1e-7 of the loss (rounds 8-48) and weighting the cells does not change that. A routing change, if ever,
  is decided by hand (for example merging e3 into e4 and splitting e6, the new expert starting from its parent's
  weights) and tested with two F runs, not with the EM.
- **The weights need accurate measurements first** (below): both distributions are estimated, and a weight is only
  as good as the noisier of its two shares.

## Measurements before the weights

What the weight is: `w(pair) = clamp(search share / training share, 0.5, 2)`, normalised to mean 1 over the training
stream, for the 64 pairs (White expert, Black expert), symmetric in the colours. On 21/10 the partial measurement gave
the scale of the effect: off-diagonal pairs 32.5% of the search evaluations against 15.8% of the stream, e3 used 1.8
times its training share, e7 0.54 times (C2_EXPERT_POPULATION_2026-10-21.md).

1. **Search distribution** (engine with `-DC2POP`: the routing cell at every network evaluation, both perspectives,
   54 x 54 histogram; `<work>\c2eng\pop\c2pop.py search`):
   - network: the P export F starts from (the distribution depends a little on the network);
   - roots: at least 2,000, from the sources the network is judged on: the UHO and 8-move books as played by the
     testers, real games after the book (as the 21/10 set), and the endgame book; the share of each source decided
     before the run;
   - depth: fixed nodes at two levels (1M and 10M per root) on a subset, to check the distribution is stable with
     depth; if it is not, use the deeper one;
   - uncertainty: bootstrap over roots (a root is the independent unit, not an evaluation). Target: relative
     standard error at most 5% for every pair with a share of at least 0.1%; pairs below that keep weight 1;
   - cost: ~2,000 roots x 1M nodes = 2e9 nodes, about 1.5-2 hours with 20 single-thread processes on the idle Xeon.
2. **Training distribution** with the F skip configuration (`<work>\c2eng\pop\c2_cell_sample.py`, the loader with
   the same flags as F): at least 10M positions over all the binpacks, bootstrap over batches; on the VM's CPU after
   P ends, or wherever the binpacks are.
3. **Weights**: the clamp and the normalisation above; report each weight with its interval, the effective sample
   size of the weighted stream, and the expected change of the per-expert training counts.
4. **Lambda per expert**: starting values 0.60 / 0.65 / 0.70 / 0.75 / 0.75 / 0.75 / 0.75 / 0.75 (e0..e7, endgame
   classes lower); before fixing them, the holdout loss per expert at lambda 1.0 / 0.75 / 0.0 of the P export
   (`c2_val_loss_fixed2.py`, split by the side to move's expert), to see where the outcome term carries information.
5. **Is every expert trained enough?** (author, 21/10: "30 billion is little if the others saw many more"). At the
   P rate an expert receives, per epoch, 57M entries (e3) to 444M (e6): over 550 epochs ~31 billion against ~245
   billion. Check per expert, on the holdout at lambda 0.75, split by the expert of each perspective:
   - the learning curve over the exports (ep9 ... ep49 ... the final one): an expert still falling when the others are
     flat, or falling more slowly per epoch, is short of data;
   - the gap of each expert's positions to Consilium's final network on the same positions (the expert where C2 lags
     most is the first candidate for a larger weight);
   - if e3 (or any expert) lags, raise its weight above the search ratio, or give it a minimum share of the stream.

## Implementation (trainer, before F)

- Loader: a skip probability per position from its pair, `p_keep = w(pair) / max w`, after the existing filters.
- Loss: lambda taken per position from the side to move's expert.
- Both behind flags, default off, so the plain F run is the same as Consilium's F3.

## Test

Two F runs from the same P checkpoint, with and without the weighting, same epochs and seed; exports at the same
epoch; SPRT between them at 20+0.2 and at fixed 100k nodes (UHO), and on the endgame book without adjudication for
the lambda part. Engine side: the eval-scale calibration per expert (`C2EvalExp*`) after the lambda change.
