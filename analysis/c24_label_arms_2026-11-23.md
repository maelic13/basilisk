**Recommendation.** Keep corpus B as C.2.8's matched arm, but expect
nothing from it. Fit both arms, which costs little, and spend the
label-contract SPRT only if the two fits differ beyond noise. The new head
labels its endings well: B changes 0.28% of train rows, and the changes run
both ways in equal numbers. A one-directional bias was the reason this
arm existed (BAS-E46), and it is gone. The main counter-argument is Rarog's
+6.73 for the same relabel (C-60). That was on an adjudicated corpus, where
truncated games mislabel far more endings than these naturally finished
ones do.

Every claim is evidence (a file read, a program run) unless marked
inference.

## 1. The question

Does replacing game results by tablebase truth, where the tablebase can
see, give corpus A better labels, and by how much? Two forms:

- **B, local** (8.4): a row of six men or fewer takes its own Syzygy
  verdict. No other row changes.
- **C, whole-game** (8.5): a game's result is replaced by the verdict at
  the first position of six men or fewer. Every sampled row of that game
  changes, the larger ones included.

## 2. The label contract, `basilisk-texel-labels-syzygy-v1` (`fbeceaf`)

`tools/texel/relabel.py` builds B from A:

- **Eligibility, row-local (8.4.e):** six men or fewer, no castling right,
  and both WDL and DTZ tables probe. Every other row keeps A's game-result
  label. A failed probe is counted as unknown, never as agreement (8.4.d);
  on corpus A, none failed and no row had a castling right.
- **The clock (8.4.c), in Stockfish's form:** take `tbprobe.cpp`
  root_probe's `r >= bound` test with the fifty-move rule on. Let `dtz` be
  the side to move's distance to zeroing and `clock` the row's rule-50
  clock. The row is a win when `dtz > 0` and `dtz + clock <= 100`, a loss
  when `dtz < 0` and `-dtz + clock <= 100`, and a draw otherwise. So cursed
  wins and blessed losses are draws, and so is a clean result the clock has
  run out on. The row's FEN carries its clock, because the de-duplication
  key keeps it (C-8).
- **The files:** B has A's three splits and rows in A's order. Every
  column but `result` is identical, and its manifest names A's by hash
  (8.4.d, 8.4.f). `check` proves all of this and re-probes every seventh
  row. B reads as an ordinary `basilisk-texel-rows-v1` corpus, so the
  tuner and `claim_test.py` take it unchanged.
- **Tests:** `tools/texel/test_relabel.py` (11 tests, in CI). They check
  the clock rule's boundaries, signs and the ineligible cases on a fake
  tablebase. On the local tables they check build, determinism, refusal to
  overwrite, and `check` catching a changed column, a relabelled large row
  and a wrong verdict.

## 4. The label error is now small

- **C-62:** on 20,000 corpus A games, 2.54% of clean tablebase wins
  reached were not won (C.2.3). BAS-E46's 19.77% was 1.9.3 at the same
  8,000 nodes.
- **Whole-game** (`relabel.py games`, every game):
  - 553,735 of 1,000,000 games reach six men or fewer, and 24,099 (4.35%)
    end against the verdict at that first position.
  - The contradictions run both ways: 13,223 games entered a drawn ending
    and were decided, and 10,833 entered a won or lost one and did not
    finish it.
- **Inference.** BAS-E46's case was a one-directional error: won endings
  labelled draws teach the evaluator to undervalue the advantages that win
  endgames. Here the error has no such direction, and it touches 0.28% of
  rows. A refit on B should move the endgame terms by about their noise.

## 5. Whole-game adjudication (8.5): no arm now

- **8.5.a, lineage.** A whole-game relabel changes every sampled row of a
  contradicted game. On corpus A that is 97,274 train rows (2.58%), of
  which 62,259 have more than six men, the rows local relabelling cannot
  reach (C-63). The semantics are sound (inference): the entry verdict is
  the game's result with the play after entry made perfect, a label of the
  same kind as a game result. This is not the datagen change of
  terminating games at the tablebase (C-64); that would also change which
  positions are sampled.
- **8.5.b, isolation.** A post-hoc relabel of corpus A's own games keeps
  the starts, the games, the extraction and every non-label column. So the
  confound 8.5.d names does not arise, and corpus C could be built in
  minutes with `relabel.py`.
- **8.5.c and 8.5.d, the verdict: no arm, for power, not for confound.**
  - C would change 2.6% of rows, with errors in both directions, and B's
    0.28% are themselves near null (§4). Inference: the prize is a few Elo
    at most, below what an SPRT of a label contract resolves at reasonable
    cost.
  - Small gains wait for Phase H, so no arm is registered.
  - BAS-O11 records the verdict and its retry trigger.

## 6. For C.2.8 (the maintainer's decision)

C.2.8 says "matched whole-surface fits of A and B, then the registered
label-contract gate". The recommendation is to keep the fits but make the
gate conditional:

- **Fits:** A and B under the same K, schedule and seed, on train.
  Validation loss is read on both arms' labels, by band, with the
  directional screen on the endgame terms.
- **The SPRT** for the label contract runs only if the two fitted vectors
  differ beyond fit noise: an endgame term group moving by more than its
  run-to-run spread, read from the learning curve's nested fits.
  Otherwise, A is the corpus and B is recorded as null at fit level.
- **The counter-argument:** a fit-level null is not an Elo null. But the
  rows changed are so few, and so balanced, that an Elo effect without a
  fit-level effect would need a mechanism none of §3's numbers show.

This changes C.2.8's registered gate, so it waits for the maintainer.
