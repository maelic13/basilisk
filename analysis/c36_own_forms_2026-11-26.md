## 1. The question

C.3 put the evaluation in classical Stockfish's form (`9587eeeb`) and
fitted every value as Basilisk's own. The maintainer's direction of
2026-11-25: Basilisk's own forms return where they give strength, each on
its own read, both arms fitted (`analysis/c31_rebuild_2026-11-25.md` §1,

and fixes how each one is read.

## 2. §14's premise, corrected

**Evidence.** §14 ranked its items by BAS-O14's meters: mobility 46.0,
the piece terms 37.7 and pawn structure 33.2 above the donor's. A meter
is E(0) − E(mask): what a family is worth to its *own* evaluation,
measured by removing it from both. It does not say what Basilisk's form
would add to the donor's. BAS-O15 measured exactly that gap: the donor's
passers and king safety, metered at +125.4 and +43.1, gained −1.6 ± 12.4
on Basilisk's evaluation.

**Inference.** The meters order nothing here. No item has a prior from
them, and the screen (§6) is the first measurement of any of them.

**Evidence, from the code.** The forms compared at `99e6353`, the last
commit that compiles the retired evaluation, against the shipped
`src/eval_rb_*.h`:

- **Mobility has almost nothing left to read.** The four tables have the
  same shapes (9, 14, 15, 28), and since C.3.4 their values are
  Basilisk's own. The mobility area is the same definition, which
  Basilisk had taken from the donor (the old code's "Step 4.6b"), apart
  from one set. What remains is Basilisk's simplification of the donor's
  own form:
  - attacks on the plain occupancy, where the donor's bishops see through
    queens and its rooks through queens and their own rooks;
  - no pin restriction, where a pinned donor piece counts only along its
    pin line;
  - the area excludes only our pinned pieces, where the donor's excludes
    every piece of either colour shielding our king.
- **Pawn structure**: blocked pawns are the same form. The donor's
  connected table (rank × phalanx × opposed × supporters) contains
  Basilisk's flat connected term and its by-rank table as special cases.
  Isolated is the same form. Genuinely Basilisk's: the majority and the
  islands, which the donor lacks, and the doubled and backward
  definitions, which differ.
- **The piece terms** carry most of the real differences: six terms the
  donor lacks, and five definitions the donor writes another way (§4).
- **Threats** are the same package, apart from Basilisk's flat pawn
  threat by target type. The donor's two queen-threat terms are
  additions of its own and displace nothing.
- **Space, the phase, the tempo and the opposite-bishop scale** are
  genuine replacements. Basilisk's own last fit had left its three space
  terms at 0, 1 and −1 cp.

## 3. The four questions

1. **Mechanism.** None is claimed in advance (§2). Each candidate is a
   hypothesis that the fitted evaluation lacks information Basilisk's
   form carries:
   - an *added* term carries a feature the donor's score does not see,
     such as a wing majority, a rook on the seventh or connected rooks;
   - a *replacing* form describes a feature both see, in a way that fits
     Basilisk's search and data better than the donor's way.
2. **Interactions**, in this codebase:
   - `rb::Evaluation::value` (`src/eval_rb.h`): the order of the sum,
     both lazy exits, `winnable()`, the two tapers, the layer and the
     tempo. The phase, tempo and opposite-bishop forms change it.
   - `rb::pawn_probe` and `PawnEntry` (`src/eval_rb_pawns.h`): the
     majority and the islands are functions of the pawns, so they belong
     in the cached entry, and the ablation key must still separate them.
   - `rb::piece_terms` and `Maps` (`src/eval_rb_pieces.h`): the piece and
     mobility forms. The maps that king danger, the threats and the
     passers read must not change under any form but the one that says so.
   - `rb::king_terms` (`src/eval_rb_king.h`): king danger already counts
     the king-ring attackers; Basilisk's zone bonuses overlap that signal.
   - `rb::passer_terms` (`src/eval_rb_threats.h`): its path term already
     conditions on a rook or queen behind the passer; Basilisk's flat
     rook-behind terms overlap it.
   - `endgame_layer` and `ocb_draw_scale` (`src/eval_endgame.h`,
     `src/eval.h`): the opposite-bishop step compiles today and is
     skipped by the template flag.
   - The tuner's model (`tools/texel/tuner.cpp`, `surface.py`, `fit.py`,
     `bake.py`): every form must be modelled exactly, or the screen fits
     a function the engine does not play.
3. **Invariants** (`docs/DESIGN.md` §3), on every form:
   - the evaluation is a function of the position alone, and the caches
     hold functions of their keys;
   - `apply_endgame` and the layer keep White's point of view, and a
     draw verdict is set only by the layer's rules;
   - static scores stay below `KNOWN_WIN` unless the layer set a tier;
   - per-node code allocates nothing and takes no lock;
   - with every form off, `bench 13` stays 3,880,333 and the played hash
     fa8739a3fc205e63, at every commit.
4. **Falsifiers.** For the method, §6's control arms. For each form, its
   screen and then its SPRT.

## 5. Values

- **Seeds.** A replacing form's new slots take `99e6353`'s shipped
  values (its `src/eval_params.h` defaults), converted to units by
  × 1024 / 620, rounded to nearest. A reused slot (`Tempo`,
  `MinorBehindPawn`, `RookOnQueenFile`, `QueenInfiltration`) takes the
  converted old value too. An added term seeds at 0.
- **So an added bit at its seed is the shipped evaluation**: `bench 13`
  3,880,333 and played hash fa8739a3fc205e63 with the bit on. A nonzero
  test value must move both. That is the wire proven inert and live.
- Every value that ships is the screen's fit, as in C.3.4.

## 6. The screen

**What it asks.** Does the form, fitted, predict corpus A's held-out
results better than the shipped evaluation refitted the same way?

**Arms.** Every arm runs C.3.4's fit (`tools/run_c34_fits.py`'s common
arguments: stages `kingsafety,linear,kingsafety,polish`, `lr` 0.5,
`patience` 50, `min_delta` 1e-6, `epochs` 20,000, no regularisation, the
knob bounds of `surface_rebuild_v1.tsv`), with two differences: it
starts from the shipped vector, plus the form's seeds, and K is frozen
at C.3.4's pair (`K_eg` 3.026687, `K_mg` 0.970080). Re-fitting K would
change the loss the arms are compared on.

- **N, the null arm**: the shipped vector, no bit. Both arms fitted:
  every candidate is compared with N, not with the shipped vector, so
  neither side gets more optimisation than the other.
- **C1, C2, the positive controls**: N with the donor's `Hanging` (C1) or
  `RookOnFile` (C2) fixed at 0. They show that the screen can see a
  working term of the size it looks for.
- **The twenty candidate arms** of §4.
- **U, all twenty bits on**: a diagnostic only, recorded and never
  decisive. BAS-O14 found the families non-additive, so U says whether
  the forms interfere, not which ones help.

**The measure.** On the validation rows the tuner uses (197,277 of
corpus A's; overrides and draw verdicts out, as in the fit), the
per-row squared error at the frozen K, paired between the arm and N:
Δ = L(N) − L(arm), positive when the arm predicts better. The interval
is a bootstrap over games (`game_id`), 2,000 resamples at a fixed seed,
because positions of one game move together.

**The rule, frozen now.**
- A candidate passes when Δ ≥ δ = 2.0e-5 and the bootstrap's 95% lower
  bound is above 0.
- **Why 2.0e-5.** The only static-to-games anchor is BAS-E63 against
  BAS-E64: 2.0e-4 of validation loss came with 16.2 Elo. Taken as a
  rate, δ is about 1.6 Elo, close to the smallest gain a `[0,3]` SPRT
  can accept in a night. **Counter-argument:** that pair changed the
  fit's K, not the evaluation's information, so the rate may not hold;
  and the static screen has misranked families before (space: first in
  the screen, −2.1 in games, BAS-O14). A form that fails it may still
  gain in play. Its retry trigger (§9) covers that case.
- **Method gates**, read before any candidate:
  - |L(N) − L(shipped)| ≤ δ/2. Otherwise refitting moves the loss by
    itself as much as a form would, δ means nothing, and the leaf
    returns to research.
  - At least one of C1, C2 has −Δ > δ: removing a working term must cost
    more than δ. Otherwise the screen cannot see a term of the size it
    looks for, and the leaf returns to research.

**Speed is not in the screen.** Basilisk's mobility count, its zone and
connected rooks need plain attack sets the new arm does not compute.
The SPRT, at equal time, pays for that.

## 7. The SPRTs

- **Order.** Passing candidates run one at a time, largest Δ first.
- **Each against the current evaluation.** When a form is accepted, it is
  baked and becomes the base. Every later candidate is then refitted on
  that base and must pass the screen again, against a new null arm,
  before its SPRT. Two returned forms are never assumed to add.
- **Conditions**, as BAS-E65's: Colosseum CLI `sprt`, `[0, 3]` nElo,
  `3+0.03`, margin 20 ms, 1T, Hash 64, UHO `UHO_Lichess_4852_v1.epd` in
  random order, paired and colour-reversed, no adjudication, no
  tablebases, fault cap 0; clean PGO PEXT builds of one commit, the
  candidate with its bit on and its fitted values baked.
- **The cap** is sized in each SPRT's own entry, before its first game,
  from the drift model at the expectation its screen implies.
- **Acceptance.** H1, and the C.2.7 endgame bracket veto of PLAN §3 at
  `21k / 64k / 190k` nodes, as at BAS-E65. If the veto binds, acceptance
  is the maintainer's ruling.
- A form whose SPRT accepts H0, or reaches its cap unresolved, stays out
  (§9).

## 8. Delivery (C.3.6.2): what implementation must make true

- **One switch.** A CMake cache value `EVAL_OWN_FORMS`, a bit mask in the
  order of §4, default 0, compiled as a constant. Any combination of bits
  compiles; each bit changes only its own form.
- **At mask 0, at every commit:** `bench 13` 3,880,333, played hash
  fa8739a3fc205e63, 59 of 59 CTest, `--verify` exact.
- **Each bit alone:**
  - the tuner models it exactly: `--verify` exact on corpus A's
    validation rows, and the played hash equal to the served hash, at
    its seed;
  - colour symmetry (`tests/colour_mirror.h`);
  - an added bit at seed 0 reproduces mask 0's bench and played hash,
    and a nonzero test value moves both;
  - its slots are in the registry, free linear in the surface, and its
    replaced donor slots fixed in its arm's surface.
- **The arms' tooling, in Python:**
  - a screen tool that takes two arms' per-row played values, computes
    the paired squared error at the frozen K and the bootstrap by game,
    and refuses on a mismatched row set or a played value that differs
    from the tuner's;
  - a frozen launcher for N, C1, C2, the twenty bits and U, which refuses
    a dirty tree, skips finished arms, writes each arm under
    `tools/results/c36-screen/<arm>/`, and dry-runs cleanly.
- **The record.** Per bit: its seeds and their source lines, the slots
  added and fixed, its bench and played hash at the seed, its tests, and
  every deviation from §4 with its reason.
- Not in this leaf: any fit, any game, any value moved in the shipped
  vector.

## 9. Retry triggers

- **Failed the screen.** Re-screened on C.5.2's refit corpus: corpus A
  is the retired evaluation's games, and the rebuilt engine's own games
  are the fair population. A fit by games (C.6) that takes in the slot
  is a second trigger.
- **Rejected by its SPRT.** Closed in this programme. Phase H's
  refinements may take it if a later evaluation regime changes what it
  measures.

**δ, corrected (§6).** §6 anchored δ on BAS-E63 against BAS-E64. Those
two losses are on different metrics: 0.09294724 is `one-k` at its single
K, 0.09274536 is `k-phase` at K(phase). At a common K the pair differs by
1.13e-3, or has the opposite sign, so the anchor gives δ no Elo meaning.
What 2.0e-5 is: just above the fit's own path noise. `k-phase` Run 1 to
Run 2 moved 0.09276149 to 0.09274536, 1.6e-5, from the knob step sizes
alone. δ keeps its value with that reason. On corpus C, δ and both gates
are scaled by L_C(R) / L_A(shipped), with L_A(shipped) = 0.09274536.

**Semantics (§4, §5).**
- **R1, `TEMPO`.** It enters after `winnable()`, so `winnable` stays a
  function of the families. The mate drive reads the taper without the
  tempo, as `99e6353` did (the drive before the tempo, its `eval.cpp`
  279 to 285). The layer's input and both tapers include it. The phase
  is the arm's own: the donor's unless bit 0 is on. The clamp stays.
- **R2, `ROOK_BEHIND_PASSER`.** The passers are `pe.passed`, the shipped
  entry's set, recorded as a deviation from the old set: an added term
  must agree with the passer family on what a passer is. "Behind" is
  from the passer owner's side for both rooks. It is summed in the passer
  family, as `pawns_full` was.
- **R3, `PAWN_CLASS`.**
  1. The donor's backward flag is still computed and still drives
     `attacks_span`; only its score goes.
  2. A pawn with no neighbours always scores `Isolated`: the donor's
     doubled-isolated substitution is one of the two `Doubled` cases
     removed.
  3. Basilisk's `Backward` (no own pawn strictly behind on an adjacent
     file, its stop square attacked by an enemy pawn; a phalanx
     neighbour does not prevent it, a blocker does not cause it) scores
     outside the chain and may coincide with `Connected` or `Isolated`.
  4. `WeakUnopposed` scores once per pawn with no neighbours or
     Basilisk-backward and not opposed, with the donor's forward-file
     `opposed` kept. The old code tested the whole file: a deviation,
     recorded.
  5. `WeakLever` is unchanged.
- **R5, `ROOK_SEVENTH` withdrawn** (research, during C.3.6.2). The form
  scores a rook on its relative seventh rank with no other condition, so
  with 64-square rook tables it is the table's eight seventh-rank squares
  moved together, in both halves. The surface's rules for the retired
  registry already fixed it as collinear for that reason. It moves from
  §4's table to the not-read list ("contained in the 64-square rook
  table"), and §2's six terms the donor lacks become five. Nineteen forms
  are read: thirteen replacing (bits 0 to 12) and six added (bits 13, 14,
  16 to 19). Bit 15 is reserved: its code and slots are removed, and a
  mask that sets it fails to compile. BAS-O18 and BAS-O20 run nineteen
  candidate arms, U is the nineteen, 23 arms before P. Retry trigger: none
  for the flat form; the conditioned form (a rook on its seventh with an
  enemy pawn on that rank or the enemy king on its back rank) is already
  C.5.1.1's Candidate C.
- **R6, two forms this screen can barely see** (research, at C.3.6.3,
  before any arm). The share of validation rows a form's slots can move,
  measured at nonzero values: `PAWN_THREAT` 7,013 rows (3.6%),
  `TRAPPED_BISHOP` 123 rows (0.06%); every other form with slots moves
  10% or more. The corpus is quiet positions, and a pawn attacking a
  piece, or a bishop with no move, is rarely quiet. The pass rule does
  not change. If either fails, it is recorded as "not readable by this
  screen (activation 3.6% / 0.06%)", not as a form that carries nothing.
  The screen on corpus C is the same instrument, so §9's corpus trigger
  does not answer it either. Its retry trigger is a read at the search's
  leaves, which C.5.1.1 already plans for threats.
- **R7, what the fallback's arms are read against** (research, 2026-11-27,
  after screen A's gate 1 failed and before any arm was read). §6 compares
  every candidate with a null that had the same optimisation. After the
  fallback every arm is one refit pass from N, and the null with one
