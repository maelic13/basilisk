## Findings

### 1. The fit makes the bare null move less aggressive on all four of its moving terms (cohesion)

- **Evidence.** Every null-move term that moved, moved toward fewer or
  shallower null-move searches. The entry condition is `static_eval ≥ beta −
  per_ply·d − improving·imp + base`; the reduction is `R = red_base + d/3 +
  max((static_eval − beta)/red_eval_div, 0)`
  (`search_kernel_core.cpp` 785–790).
  - `B3NmpRedBase` 7 → 4 is the only full-step mover (−1.50; centre
    4.45, so the rounding is close to 5). It retraces three quarters of
    the way from the donor's 7 to Basilisk's fitted 3 (packet §7, flagged
    there as a seed far from Basilisk's value).
  - `B3NmpBase` 151 → 163: a stricter entry.
  - `B3NmpImproving` 16 → 15: less relaxation when improving.
  - `B3NmpRedEvalDiv` 72 → 80: a smaller eval bonus to R.
  - `B3NmpPerPly` is flat.
- **Reading.** The arm runs the donor's bare null move (`B3NmpEvalGate` 0
  after the 2026-11-02 veto). B.3.2 read the eval gate at +11.3 ± 9.3, and
  the gate also makes the null move less aggressive. The fit is doing with
  the constants part of what the gate does by form. The donor pairs `7 +
  d/3` with a tree pruned and reduced differently; on this tree the
  donor's ply value does not transfer, as §7 suspected.
- **Fix.** None to the bake: theta takes the fitted values. B.3.4's
  registered re-read of the gate (`B3NmpEvalGate` 1 against 0 on the fitted
  arm) measures exactly this combination. Its reading covers any
  over-conservatism from stacking the gate on constants that compensate for
  it.
  - **This analysis does not ask for a refit if B.3.4 adopts the gate.**
    The adoption read is taken on these constants, so it already prices
    the stack.
  - If the gate is adopted, the four null-move coordinates are B.6's
    first joint-tune case.
- **Owner:** B.3.4 (the gate re-read, registered); B.6 if adopted.

### 2. The singular margin moves down while the sweep pointed up (instrument disagreement)

- **Evidence.**
  - `B3SingMargin` 252 → 232 (−0.87 steps; its excursion touched −1.00
    at iteration 1674).
  - The sweep read WAC monotone upward to the range's top: 244 at 378
    against 240 at 252.
  - The donor's 17, at the other end of the range, read 241.
  - `B3SingMarginTtPv` follows the same way (19 → 17).
- **Reading.** Games prefer a slightly smaller margin than the seed, which
  means more singular extensions. The tactical instrument prefers fewer.
  The fit is far from the donor's 17 and from both range edges, so no rail
  hides a wanted value.
- **Fix.** None. The departure from the donor's margin stays and goes to
  B.3.4's departures table for B.6.1, with this reading.
- **Owner:** B.3.4 (departures table).

### 3. The zero-game sweep pointed the wrong way on every coordinate the fit moved (instrument)

- **Evidence.** The sweep's WAC optimum (`analysis/b33_sweep_2026-11-02.md`)
  against the fit's direction:

  | Coordinate | WAC best | Fit |
  |---|---|---|
  | `B3NmpBase` | 113 (0.75×) | 163 |
  | `B3NmpRedEvalDiv` | 54 (0.75×) | 80 |
  | `B3ProbCutBase` | 102 (1.5×) | 73 |
  | `B3SingMargin` | 378 (top) | 232 |
  | `B3SingTripleBase` | 10 (0.5×) | 20 (flat) |

  On the first four, the fit moved away from the WAC optimum.
- **Reading.** WAC at 100,000 nodes did its registered job: it showed
  curvature, the SPSA's go/no-go. It is not evidence of the game optimum's
  direction on this surface. The seeds sat at a low WAC point on four of
  the five lines (240 against 237–248), and the games moved them elsewhere.
- **Fix.** No rule change. PROCESS *SPSA go/no-go* (step 3) uses the
  sweep only to decide whether to tune. Recorded so that no later leaf
  reads a sweep's WAC maximum as a predicted fit.
- **Owner:** none (recorded).

### 4. The surface is mostly flat at STC (the competing explanation, partly)

- **Evidence.**
  - One of 29 coordinates moved a full step.
  - Eight end exactly at their seed, and 26 end within 0.75 steps.
  - Several early excursions returned to the seed: `CoreContHistPruneSlope`
    +0.51 at iteration 382 → +0.02; `B3SingTriplePv` +0.41 → −0.20;
    `B3SingDoubleBase` +0.33 → 0.00; `CoreRfpDepthCap` −0.33 → 0.00.
    These are the large early gains, not reversals of a trend.
- **Reading.** BAS-S42's competing explanation ("the surface is flat at
  STC for most coordinates") holds for most of the surface. The null move
  (finding 1) and the singular margin (finding 2) are the exceptions.
  Whether the fit is worth anything is BAS-S43 (b)'s question, the fitted
  arm against the unfitted, and the gate answers it, not this analysis.
- **Owner:** B.3.5 (b).

### 5. The Cluster 1 admissions barely move (design input for B.6)

- **Evidence.** None of the ten §5.12 admissions moved a full step. The
  largest movers:
  - `CoreLmpBase` 3 → 4 (+0.50; centre 4.13): fewer late-move prunes;
  - `CoreCorrUpdateScale` 16 → 17 (+0.50);
  - `CoreCaptFutBase` 79 → 74 (−0.36): more capture futility pruning;
  - `CoreRfpCorrMult` 538 → 498 (−0.31).
  - The RFP trio is flat or near it.
- **Reading.** §5.12's activation rule admitted these coordinates because
  the ON arm moved their populations by at least 10%. In games, their
  B.2.3 values remain near-optimal on the new tree. Packet §5's B.6 clause
  makes this read the measured case for B.6's joint tune: no admission
  crossed a full step.
- **Owner:** B.6 (its skip decision).

### 6. One edge, by design: `B3SingTripleQuiet` (no action)

- **Evidence.** `B3SingTripleQuiet` is seeded at its range maximum 0, which

- **Reading.** The edge does not hide a value; the range is one-sided on
  purpose so the fit could restore the donor's −53. It restored very little.
- **Owner:** none.

### 7. IIR's minimum depth drifts down late (cohesion, consistent)

- **Evidence.** `B3IirMinDepth` 6 → 5 (centre 5.38, −0.50 steps). The
  movement came late: +0.03 steps at iteration 1000, −0.40 at 1817.
- **Reading.** More IIR, the same direction as B.3.2's IIR-off read
  (−11.3 ± 9.2 against keeping it). The donor's 6 sits one rounding away.
- **Owner:** none.

### 8. The bake cannot touch only the ON arm as registered (structure; the maintainer's decision)

- **Evidence.**
  - BAS-S42 and packet §11 register the bake as "one commit with the ON
    arm's new fingerprint".
  - Theta changes seven Cluster 1 defaults (`CoreLmpBase`,
    `CoreQuietFutBase`, `CoreCaptFutBase`, `CoreContHistPruneSlope`,
    `CoreRfpCorrMult`, `CoreCorrUpdateScale`, `CoreRfpPerPly`). Their X
    lines in `src/search_params.h` are shared by both arms, so writing
    them there also moves the OFF arm, the fingerprint record
    (11,880,564).
  - BAS-S43 (a) gates the fitted ON arm against the OFF arm at the bake
    revision. Its *reject* clause restores the fingerprint record.
- **Options.**
  - **(A) Per-arm defaults for the seven, OFF unchanged.** The ON
    (`BASILISK_B3_PROOF`) build takes theta; the OFF build keeps its B.2.3
    values. One fingerprint moves, as registered. BAS-S43 (a)'s OFF arm
    is the same arm B.3.2's paired run read, and a reject leaves the OFF
    arm untouched.
    - Cost: a small engine change to the defaults table (an arm-selected
      default for those seven lines), plus a test that each arm advertises
      its own default.
    - When the cluster lands and the OFF arm retires, the ON values become
      the only ones.
  - **(B) Shared bake.** Both arms take the seven values, and both
    fingerprints move.
    - The OFF arm then carries Cluster 1 constants fitted on the ON arm's
      tree, so BAS-S43 (a) no longer compares against B.3.2's OFF arm.
    - Every reference to 11,880,564 (AGENTS, the fingerprint record)
      changes with it.
- **Recommendation: (A).** It is what the registration says, and it keeps
  (a)'s control arm unchanged. The movements are small (finding 5), so
  (B)'s shift of the control would be small too, but it is not measured,
  and (A) costs a few lines.
- **Owner:** B.3.3's bake, after the maintainer's decision.
